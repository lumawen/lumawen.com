Privacy

Last updated November 9, 2026

Lumawen is a small, open source project. We collect as little as we can, we never sell it, and we don't use ad trackers. The code that handles your data is public on GitHub, so you can check everything here.

Mailing list

When you sign up for updates we store your email address, when you signed up and confirmed, and whether you're subscribed. We only email you after you click the confirm link. Every email has an unsubscribe link that works with one click. If you never confirm, we delete the sign-up after 30 days.

Accounts

If you make a Lumawen account we store your email address, an optional display name, and a securely hashed password (we can't read it). We keep track of signed-in sessions so you can sign out everywhere.

If you sign in with Google, Google tells us your email address, whether Google has verified it, your name (used as your display name) and a Google account ID. We only ask for your basic profile and email, and we use them only to sign you in. We don't get access to your Gmail, contacts or anything else in your Google account.

Security logs

To stop password guessing and spam we keep short-lived counters of sign-in and sign-up attempts by IP address and by email address, which we delete within about a day. Copies of the emails we send you (confirm links, newsletters) are deleted 30 days after sending. Our server keeps standard web logs (IP address, page, time) for troubleshooting.

Who else sees it

Our server runs on our own hardware. Emails are sent through our email provider, Purelymail. Google sees that you used Google sign-in, if you do. That's it: no analytics, ad networks or data brokers.

Your choices

Unsubscribe from any email with its link. To see, correct or delete everything we have about you, email hello@lumawen.com and we'll take care of it. Unsubscribing also cancels any newsletter still waiting to go out to you. If you have an account you can also remove Google sign-in by resetting your password.

Changes

If this page changes in a way that matters, we'll say so on the mailing list. Its history is in our public repository.
